Visualising the Chargefox network coverage.

The purpose of this map is to visualise the network coverage of the Chargefox network across greater Australia using isochrone mapping.

Isochrone mapping is a way of visualising distance x time between multiple points.

Each shaded area illustrates the distance a Chargefox user can travel on public roads using the Chargefox network. This includes all direct hosted and roaming stations.

The chosen vehicle specs are a rounded mean of the most populous EV models in Australia (Tesla Model 3, Tesla Model Y, BYD Atto 3, MG 4) with ~60 kWh battery with typical highway efficiency (~170 Wh/km).

Darker shading means higher connector density available in that area.

    How this map works

    The methodology used in this model is conservative charging behaviour seen in rural long distance driving, as the primary purpose of this visualisation is to illustrate coverage over vast areas with lower connector density.

    The formula applied to each connector is power_delivery_kW × charge_duration, applied to the vehicle model (max_soc − reserve_soc) × battery_size / highway_efficiency.

    A charge stop will calculate up to the set charge time or battery capacity, whichever comes first.

    Connector parameters:
    power_kW = rated power delivery of a connector
    charge_duration = 15, 30 or 60 minutes

    Vehicle parameters:
    Max_soc = 100%
    Reserve_soc on arrival = 10%
    Battery_size = 60 kWh
    highway_efficiency = 170 Wh/km

    Total distance possible on a full charge:
    0.9 × 60 / 0.170 = 317.6 km
